For a network of slender acoustic waveguides of **equal length**, the pressure at the junctions obeys a discrete equation that is *exactly* a lattice model: the acoustic frequency enters through a pseudo-energy such as <i>E</i> = cos(<i>kℓ</i>), and the couplings are fixed by the **cross-sections** of the tubes. No tight-binding approximation, fitting parameter or resonant element is needed, and the correspondence holds over a broad frequency range. With this mapping we realised acoustic analogues of **graphene**, with Helmholtz resonators breaking inversion symmetry and opening valley-type gaps, and of the **Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (SSH)** chain, whose edge mode is pinned at the quarter-wavelength condition and survives strong cross-section disorder in experiments. Extensions include the trimer SSH3 chain, subwavelength edge modes with resonator-loaded segments, two-dimensional SSH networks with edge and **corner modes** robust to disorder, **topologically invisible defects** in Kekulé honeycomb networks combining chiral and mirror symmetry, and topological states protected by **hidden (latent) symmetry**.
