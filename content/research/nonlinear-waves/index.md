<p class="text-muted small">Figure: ring-shaped acoustic solitons in a square lattice of acoustic waveguides. From I. Ioannou Sougleridis <i>et al.</i>, Phys. Rev. E <b>111</b>, 044201 (2025), Fig. 5.</p>

High-amplitude waves in lattices and waveguides can balance dispersion with nonlinearity and propagate as **solitons**. We study acoustic solitons in waveguides loaded with Helmholtz resonators or membranes, in periodic waveguides (theory and experiments) and in two-dimensional networks, where ring-shaped solitons appear. In **disordered** lattices we investigate how nonlinearity and chaos break Anderson localization and drive the spreading of wave packets in granular, phononic and soft architected structures. More recently, we combine nonlinearity with **nonreciprocity**: nonlinear skin modes, edge solitons that are insensitive to the boundaries, and solitonic pulses that tame the growth of the non-Hermitian skin effect.
